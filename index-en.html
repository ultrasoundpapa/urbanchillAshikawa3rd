01Profile

Not three titles.
One job with three exits.

Take something complicated apart, and set it back down in a form the other person can actually handle. Asked what I do, that is the whole answer.

Beside an operating room, that work is called implementation design, or building consensus. Rebuilding eight bars, it is called arrangement. Cutting a line, revision. Only the name changes; the hand does not. How much weight can I take off the person receiving this — that is the test, every time.

So the clinical floor is not the opposite of creative work for me. Because my weekdays are spent closing off room for misreading, I rarely hesitate about where to leave room open in the other work.

02Expertise

The floor, the sound, the words.

I draw no line between a main job and a side one. All three are taken on by the same standard.

Field

Bringing devices
onto the floor.

As a sales representative for a global medical device manufacturer, I stand between physicians, technologists, procurement and management — four groups who speak four different languages. The job is not done at the sale; it is done when the device is in use.

  • Product briefings, evidence review, in-hospital proposal design
  • Cross-functional consensus and approval support
  • Post-installation onboarding and workflow adoption
  • Long-term relationships with regional institutions

Sound

Making music
alongside AI.

I use generative AI as a tool and run everything from lyrics to final mix myself. The point is not to hand the work over to the machine — it is to own the editorial decisions of what to keep and what to throw away.

  • Original tracks, written and released
  • BGM and sound logos for spaces, film and events
  • Tonal direction matched to a brand's world
  • Sharing practical AI production workflows

Words

Keeping the days
in language.

Through essays and a podcast, I write and talk about working, making, and living in Hokkaido. I try not to rush to a conclusion, and to leave the uncertain middle of things intact.

  • An ongoing essay series on note
  • Podcast planning, recording and release
  • Contributions, interviews, conversations
  • Articulating concepts for companies and brands
Through-line
The habit the clinical floor taught me — separating fact from inference before speaking — became, unchanged, the way I write. The instinct music taught me, that you finish a thing by subtraction, is what I use to build a proposal deck. It feels less like moving between three worlds than like using one set of tools in three places. Being in Hokkaido, at a distance from the center, is part of why this way of working was possible at all.
